The crypto fear and greed index, explained

Sentiment gauges compress many signals into one number from 0 to 100. Useful as context, risky as a trading rule.

Crypto5 min readLast updated October 11, 2026By kryptooo Editorial. How we write

On this page

  1. The idea
  2. Typical inputs
  3. How the kryptooo gauge is built
  4. Reading the zones
  5. Using it responsibly
Key takeaways
  • Low scores mean fear, high scores mean greed, and the middle is neutral.
  • Different providers use different inputs, so scores are not comparable across sites.
  • Sentiment describes the present mood. It does not forecast the future.

The idea

Markets are driven partly by numbers and partly by emotion. A fear and greed index tries to measure the emotional side. Scores near 0 indicate extreme fear, near 100 indicate extreme greed, and the middle of the range is neutral. The usual shorthand is that fear can push prices below what fundamentals justify, while greed can push them above.

Typical inputs

Publicly available versions of the gauge generally blend several signals. Common ones include price volatility compared with recent averages, market momentum and trading volume, social media activity, survey data, the share of the market held by the largest asset, and search trends. Each provider chooses its own inputs and weights, so a score on one site can differ from a score on another.

How the kryptooo gauge is built

The gauge on our crypto page is computed from the twelve assets we track. It averages five components, each scaled from 0 to 100:

ComponentWhat it looks atWeight
MomentumAverage 24-hour price change across the tracked coins30%
BreadthShare of coins trading above their opening price20%
VolatilityHow large the average move is; calmer markets score higher20%
DominanceBitcoin's share of total market cap; a rising share is read as risk aversion15%
VolumeTrading volume relative to its typical level15%

Because kryptooo's data is simulated for demonstration, the gauge reflects that simulation and not the real market.

Reading the zones

  • 0 to 24, extreme fear: broad selling and elevated volatility.
  • 25 to 44, fear: caution outweighs optimism.
  • 45 to 54, neutral: no strong lean.
  • 55 to 74, greed: rising prices and confidence.
  • 75 to 100, extreme greed: strong rallies and crowded positioning.

Using it responsibly

Some investors read extremes as contrarian signals, on the theory that panic creates bargains and euphoria precedes pullbacks. Others use sentiment to confirm a trend. Both approaches can fail: markets can stay fearful or greedy for long stretches. Treat the gauge as one input among many, and never as a standalone reason to trade.
