Diversification basics

The oldest rule in investing is also one of the most misunderstood. Here is what diversification does, and what it does not.

Investing basics5 min readLast updated October 11, 2026By kryptooo Editorial. How we write

On this page

  1. The core idea
  2. Correlation
  3. Layers of diversification
  4. Concentration risk
  5. Rebalancing
  6. What diversification cannot do
Key takeaways
  • Diversification reduces the impact of any single holding, not market risk itself.
  • Assets that move differently from each other offer the most benefit.
  • Rebalancing keeps your mix aligned with your plan.

The core idea

Diversification means spreading money across different investments so that no single one can sink your whole portfolio. If one company stumbles, the others can offset part of the loss. The goal is not to maximize returns but to improve the balance between return and risk.

Correlation

Correlation measures how two assets move relative to each other, on a scale from -1 to +1. A value near +1 means they tend to rise and fall together. Near 0 means little relationship. Negative values mean they tend to move in opposite directions. Holding ten assets that are all highly correlated provides far less protection than holding a few that are not.

Layers of diversification

  • Across asset classes: for example equities, bonds, cash and, for some investors, a small allocation to alternatives or digital assets.
  • Within an asset class: multiple companies, sectors and company sizes instead of one stock.
  • Geographic: exposure to more than one country or region.
  • Over time: investing at regular intervals rather than all at once, sometimes called dollar-cost averaging.

Concentration risk

The opposite of diversification is concentration. A portfolio dominated by one stock, one sector or one asset can deliver outsized gains, and outsized losses. Cap-weighted index funds can themselves become concentrated if a few companies grow to dominate the index, so it is worth looking at what a fund actually holds.

Rebalancing

Over time, winners grow and losers shrink, so your mix drifts from your plan. Rebalancing means periodically selling some of what has grown and buying what has lagged to restore your target weights. Some investors rebalance on a schedule, such as once a year, and others when an allocation drifts by a set amount. Taxes and trading costs matter here, so consider them.

What diversification cannot do

It does not guarantee a profit or protect against loss in a broad downturn. In a severe market stress, correlations between many assets can rise together, which reduces the benefit just when you want it. Diversification manages specific risk. It cannot remove market risk.

Not advice

This is general education. The right mix depends on your goals, time horizon, and tolerance for risk. A licensed financial adviser can help you decide.
