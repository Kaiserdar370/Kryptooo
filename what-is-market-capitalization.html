What is market capitalization?

Market cap is the quickest way to compare the size of companies and digital assets, and one of the easiest numbers to misread.

Fundamentals4 min readLast updated October 11, 2026By kryptooo Editorial. How we write

On this page

  2. Why price alone is misleading
  3. Size categories
  4. Float-adjusted market cap
  5. Market cap in crypto
  6. What market cap does not tell you
Key takeaways
  • Market cap equals price multiplied by the number of shares or coins in circulation.
  • It measures size, not value: a larger company is not automatically a better investment.
  • For crypto, circulating supply and fully diluted value can differ a lot.

The formula

For a listed company, market capitalization = share price × shares outstanding. A company with 200 million shares trading at 50 has a market cap of 10 billion. Because the share price changes all day, so does market cap.

Why price alone is misleading

A 500 share price does not make a company bigger than one priced at 50. A company can have few shares at a high price or many shares at a low price. Market cap removes that distortion by accounting for the share count, which is why indices and fund managers use it rather than price.

Size categories

Providers group companies into large-cap, mid-cap and small-cap tiers. The boundaries vary by index provider and change over time, so treat any single threshold as a convention rather than a rule. In general, larger companies are more established and trade more actively. Smaller companies can grow faster but tend to be more volatile and harder to trade.

Float-adjusted market cap

Some shares are held by insiders or governments and rarely trade. Float-adjusted market cap counts only the shares available to the public. Many major indices weight companies by float-adjusted market cap because it better reflects what investors can actually buy.

Market cap in crypto

For digital assets, market cap is price multiplied by circulating supply, the coins currently available. Two related figures are worth knowing:

  • Total supply: coins that exist now, including some that may be locked or reserved.
  • Maximum supply: the hard cap, if the protocol has one.
  • Fully diluted valuation: price multiplied by the maximum supply, which shows the implied size if every coin were released.

An asset with a small circulating supply and a large maximum supply can look cheap on market cap while being expensive on a fully diluted basis.

What market cap does not tell you

  • It is not the amount of money invested in the asset. Trades at the margin set the price for everything outstanding.
  • It does not account for debt. Two companies with the same market cap can have very different balance sheets.
  • It says nothing about whether the price is fair. For that you need earnings, cash flow and growth, or in crypto, usage and supply dynamics.
